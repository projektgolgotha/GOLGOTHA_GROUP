Start with understanding

Your first step is a reading record.

Choose a translation you can legally access and read Genesis 1:1–2:3. Record the edition, passage, and source. Then write 150–200 words that distinguish three observations from one interpretation. Bring one question you want to investigate.

The course pathway

One connected study of the canon.

The map follows the Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church’s published list: 46 Old Testament entries and 35 New Testament entries. Some entries combine books or distinguish parts of a larger work. Open each course below to see the assigned entries and related video resources.

This is a developing survey pathway. Video links are related teaching resources; primary-text selections and assessed course coverage await academic review. Study hours are planning estimates for reading, viewing, writing, and feedback.

SEM-199Canon Studies Capstone24 estimated study hours

Defend an evidence portfolio, compare canonical traditions, and present a reviewed teaching project.

Course outline and resource map

Build a portfolio of your reading records, compare canonical traditions using cited sources, and prepare a teaching project that you can explain and revise after feedback. The assessment plan includes a written project and an oral discussion.

The current design estimates 420 total study hours across the pathway. Workload estimates will be refined during an assessed pilot. See the academic program and assessment design.

From study to service

Demonstrate what you understand.

For each book or component

  1. Read: identify the assigned text, edition, and passage.
  2. Observe: describe its language, structure, and context.
  3. Interpret: make a claim supported by the passage and cited sources.
  4. Apply: explain an implication for teaching, practice, or service.
  5. Revise: respond to questions and feedback.

For the final portfolio

Bring together reading records across the canon, a comparative study, and a teaching project. The proposed assessment uses clear criteria for textual understanding, evidence, reasoning, application, and revision.
