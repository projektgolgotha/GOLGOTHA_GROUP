# SEM-199 — Canon Studies Capstone

**Version:** 0.1. **Format:** proposed noncredit capstone. **Planning estimate:** 24 study hours. This guide is a teaching design for academic adoption within ECS-300; completion of the full certificate also requires its other adopted ECS outcomes, including the history component.

## Purpose

Show that you can identify the texts you study, build an interpretation from evidence, compare canonical traditions fairly, and teach a well-defined idea. Your work should make your sources and reasoning visible to another reader.

## Your submission

### 1. Canon comparison essay — 1,500–2,000 words

Compare the adopted Ethiopian church inventory with one other documented canonical inventory. Identify both sources and their institutional or historical contexts. Explain at least three consequential differences, including one grouping/numbering difference and one question of text identity. Avoid treating every difference in spelling or English title as a different work.

Choose one passage from a work central to your comparison. Explain how a reader's understanding of its genre, edition, and place in a tradition affects an interpretation. Support your argument with the primary passage, at least two suitable scholarly sources, and the inventories themselves. Represent the other tradition in terms its informed adherents could recognize.

End with one unresolved question and a realistic research step. Do not claim that a comparison proves one institution endorses the Seminary.

### 2. Teaching demonstration — 12–15 minutes

Choose an adult learning audience and write one measurable learning outcome. Prepare a teaching session using at least two primary passages and a short check of understanding. Submit a one-page facilitator guide with the outcome, sequence, source references, discussion prompt, and check. Deliver or record the lesson through the agreed route.

The session should be understandable without assuming the audience has watched your source videos. Distinguish the primary passage, the tradition you are describing, and your interpretation. Explain how the audience's context shaped your examples.

### 3. Portfolio and source record

Submit the reviewed canon-unit index, selected examples of your strongest and most revised work, and an annotated bibliography. Each bibliography entry should state what the source contributes and one relevant limitation. Include the adopted editions, URLs/page references, and your acknowledgment of assistance. The reviewer checks the unit index against actual retained submissions; an index alone is not evidence of completed work.

### 4. Conversation and revision

Attend a 15-minute instructor conversation or agreed accessible equivalent. Be prepared to explain a source choice, reconstruct the reasoning behind an interpretation, discuss an alternative, and identify a revision. After feedback, submit a 300–500-word revision note explaining the changes made and any remaining disagreement with reasons.

## Capstone rubric

Each dimension contributes equally: **0–3 points, five dimensions, maximum 15**. Read the descriptors before submitting.

| Dimension | 0 — Missing/unreliable | 1 — Developing | 2 — Competent | 3 — Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Canon and text identity | Inventories or works are absent or materially misidentified | Lists some differences but confuses a key grouping or work | Accurate comparison distinguishes numbering, works, and selected editions | Explains why the differences matter and handles ambiguous identities with documented limits |
| Interpretation and argument | Unsupported conclusion or no primary passage analysis | An argument is present but context or genre is weakly handled | Coherent claim supported by close reading, context, and a considered alternative | Tests competing readings, qualifies conclusions, and connects evidence precisely |
| Research and traceability | Fabricated/untraceable sources or unacknowledged substitution | Sources can partly be located, but edition or claim links are incomplete | Primary and scholarly sources are traceable and appropriately used | Critically evaluates source quality, translation limits, and uncertainty across the argument |
| Teaching and fair representation | No usable lesson or substantial misrepresentation | Lesson has content but a vague outcome, weak learning check, or one-sided comparison | Clear audience/outcome, contextual teaching, fair comparison, and a suitable check | Responds to learner evidence, anticipates misunderstanding, and adapts the lesson thoughtfully |
| Explanation and revision | Learner cannot explain the work or supplies no substantive response | Explains some choices but does not use feedback meaningfully | Explains decisions and makes a reasoned revision with appropriate acknowledgment | Integrates critique, defends justified disagreement, and identifies a useful next inquiry |

**Proposed passing standard:** at least **12/15**, with **no dimension below 2**. Provide one planned revision opportunity after feedback. A second qualified reviewer independently scores the capstone; reviewers discuss any dimension differing by more than one point and record the final rationale. This moderation design is a program proposal, not a claim that reviewers are already appointed.

## Instructor record

Record the curriculum/rubric version, learner ID, submission locations, first and second reviewer IDs, separate initial scores, moderation notes, feedback date, revision, final scores, and decision. Link the result to the relevant PLOs: canon literacy, interpretation, Ethiopian context where addressed, research integrity, teaching, and reflective practice.

Before a full ECS-300 award decision, verify the distinct ECS-302 history assessment and all other adopted program requirements. A capstone pass cannot fill an unresolved required course by assumption. The public verification record, if later issued by an authorized institutional process, should disclose the actual award and issuer without publishing the learner's private assessment portfolio.
