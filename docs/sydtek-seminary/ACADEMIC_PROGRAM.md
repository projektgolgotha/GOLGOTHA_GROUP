# SydTek Seminary — Ethiopian Canon Studies academic design

**Version:** 0.1. **Prepared:** 30 October 2026. **Status:** authored development proposal. No faculty approval, learner grading, institutional authorization, accreditation, or credential issuance is asserted by this document.

## Place in the existing Seminary

The existing [SydTek Seminary curriculum index](https://github.com/GoldstonianConcordanceBible/SydTek-Seminary/blob/main/SydTek_Seminary/CURRICULUM_INDEX.md) identifies four certificate pathways. This build develops the existing **ECS-300 Certificate in Ethiopian Canon Studies**, whose [program draft](https://github.com/GoldstonianConcordanceBible/SydTek-Seminary/blob/main/SydTek_Seminary/PROGRAMS/CERTIFICATES/CERTIFICATE_IN_ETHIOPIAN_CANON_STUDIES.md) emphasizes canon history, expanded biblical literacy, and portfolio assessment. Its six ECS course codes remain intact. The SEM codes below provide a delivery structure for the 81-entry survey and supporting assessments.

Existing GCB/ENOCH playlists, including their published module names, remain source materials with their own identifiers. The curriculum maps them to outcomes after text and content review; it does not erase or rename their source identities. GCB interpretations should be identified as the instructor's or program's interpretations where appropriate. Adopting an Ethiopian biblical inventory does not establish that every teaching claim represents Ethiopian Orthodox doctrine or that the Church endorses the Seminary.

## Proposed learner pathway

Begin with a noncredit pilot of SEM-000 and SEM-101: 48 estimated study hours, a complete orientation, eight book worksheets, an interpretation essay, and a teaching demonstration. Review the workload and quality of learner work before opening the remaining sequence as assessed courses. Public access to a reading library can precede assessed-cohort readiness.

The initial assessed pilot is designed for adult learners. Any later K–12 offering needs its own age-appropriate pedagogy, safeguarding, consent, and learner-support design; it is not created by copying this adult Seminary curriculum.

## Assessment and rubric

Use `UNIT_WORKSHEET.md` for each of the 81 entries. For entries with multiple components, the instructor specifies the required components before teaching begins. Keep **content coverage** and **individual learner attainment** as separate records. A curriculum can contain a lesson while a learner has not demonstrated its outcome; a learner can watch a video without the course meeting a required textual scope.

| Criterion | 0 — Missing/unreliable | 1 — Developing | 2 — Competent | 3 — Strong |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Textual accuracy | No assessable reading, or central claims contradict the cited passage | Some correct observations with consequential omissions | Accurate observations and references support the main claim | Precise reading attends to relevant details and qualifies the claim |
| Interpretation and context | No reasoning connects text and conclusion | Reasoning relies mainly on assertion or ignores genre/context | A coherent argument uses genre and immediate context | Weighs alternative readings and explains the effect of context |
| Evidence and traceability | Sources are absent, fabricated, or unidentifiable | Some usable references; edition or claim-source links incomplete | Primary edition and key claims are traceable | Sources are evaluated, limitations acknowledged, and comparisons reproducible |
| Fair comparison and application | Misrepresents another view or applies the text without reasoning | A comparison or application is asserted with little support | Represents another view fairly and explains the application | Tests the scope and limits of application with careful ethical reasoning |
| Communication and reflection | Work cannot be evaluated or is not the learner's own | Meaning is partly clear; feedback not substantively used | Clear structure, appropriate acknowledgment, and a reasoned revision | Concise teaching, transparent reasoning, and a significant improvement after critique |

For an assessed survey course, propose an overall threshold of 80%, all required unit submissions, and at least 2/3 in textual accuracy and evidence/traceability on each final unit record. Provide feedback and a planned revision opportunity. Use course-specific task weights, such as those in SEM-101, declared before assessment.

For a future complete ECS-300 award, require all adopted ECS-301–306 outcomes, all required survey unit records, the history assessment, and the capstone. The capstone includes a 1,500–2,000-word comparative canon essay, a 12–15-minute teaching demonstration, an annotated source list, and a 15-minute instructor conversation. Proposed capstone threshold: 12/15 with no rubric criterion below 2. A second reviewer moderates the capstone and a sample of unit work. These quantities are program design proposals, not accreditor-mandated numbers.

Assess scholarly reasoning, accurate representation, and the stated learning outcome. Do not use agreement with an unstated theological preference as a grading criterion. Publish any confessional learning expectations explicitly. An oral review may use an accessible alternative agreed with the learner.

## Instruction, learner support, and academic records

For the pilot, schedule an instructor-led passage discussion each week, a weekly opportunity for questions, and individual feedback within seven calendar days. Confirm the staffing needed to meet those commitments before offering an assessed cohort. Track intervention when a learner stops participating or shows a persistent misunderstanding; peer discussion complements instructor teaching and evaluation.

Skool can host the classroom sequence and moderated community. ENOCH WORLD provides public discovery and program information. The academic record needs a controlled system containing learner ID, consent, course/version, required readings, submissions, rubric results, reviewer, feedback, revision, decision, and any issued credential ID. Do not publish student assessments or personal pastoral disclosures in the public repository.

At the end of the pilot, analyze completion, withdrawals, outcome achievement, workload variation, accessibility problems, and learner feedback. Report denominators and the cohort period. The supplied 4,195,671-view screenshot is creator-provided audience evidence for a selected 365-day period; it is not a measure of these academic outcomes.

## From noncredit study toward degree capability

1. **Pilot:** adopt the orientation and first course, appoint an instructor, resolve required readings, and collect real evaluated work.
2. **Complete certificate design:** resolve all six existing ECS requirements, confirm the 81-unit reading crosswalk, create the remaining assessments, and establish the issuer and records process.
3. **Develop a legal degree pathway:** determine the entity and jurisdictions; secure applicable authorization/exemption; adopt admissions, credit, faculty, library, governance, finance, and student-protection arrangements.
4. **Choose an accreditor by mission and actual eligibility:** prepare the required operating evidence, self-study, peer review, and any prescribed membership/application stages. Accreditation can be claimed only after an applicable formal decision.

A future bachelor's design would require general education and a complete undergraduate program beyond this canon survey. A future graduate design would require graduate admissions expectations, advanced research and language resources, appropriately qualified faculty, and program-level theological/ministry formation. A doctorate would require a separate research-supervision and scholarly-production capacity. None of those degrees is established by these course shells.

Academic credits must be assigned through an approved institutional policy with demonstrated learning, appropriate workload, and review. Do not divide the 420-hour estimate by a convenient conversion factor and label the result transferable credit. See `ACCREDITATION_PATHWAY.md` for the researched eligibility and authorization distinctions.
