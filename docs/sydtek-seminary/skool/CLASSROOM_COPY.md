COUNTING UNITS AND COMPONENT TEXTS

The unit IDs are OT-01 through OT-46 and NT-01 through NT-35. A registry entry is a counting unit: OT-09 includes both 1 and 2 Samuel. Entries do not all have the same length or difficulty. The course record must retain the identity of every component text.

The English list’s Maccabees labels must be connected to Ethiopic Meqabyan texts, not substituted with the Greek books of Maccabees. See the United Bible Societies translation resource:

https://tips.translation.bible/story/the-book-of-1-meqabyan/

Four Sinodos entries and two Covenant entries require attention to the individual part being studied. A general church-order playlist does not automatically document all those parts. Supplemental historical and comparative materials have their own labels.

## SEM-000.3 | A traceable reading record

https://www.skool.com/ai-at-work-101-7709/classroom/4fca2aef?md=abcd0b752fbd44a1ad2fd400a46390b4

BUILD A TRACEABLE READING RECORD

Suggested time: two hours.

FOUR LAYERS OF READING

TEXT — the work and passage you are studying.

EDITION OR TRANSLATION — the specific form of that work you can inspect.

INTERPRETATION — an explanation or argument about the passage.

APPLICATION — how you propose to respond in life, teaching or ministry.

A video is a teaching source. Check its claims against a primary passage and a suitable reference. If a video has no source for a historical claim, record that claim as needing verification. Where translations differ, quote only what is necessary, identify the edition and explain the effect of the difference.

ACTIVITY — GENESIS 1:1–2:3

Use a legally accessible translation that you own or can access. Record:

1. Work and passage range.

2. Translation title and translator or publishing body.

3. Edition and year, if supplied.

4. URL or page numbers and access date.

5. Three observations about the passage.

6. One interpretation, clearly distinguished from those observations.

7. One question you cannot yet answer.

Write 150–200 words for your observations and interpretation. Use a repeated phrase, a pattern of actions or a stated distinction as evidence. Explain how your interpretation follows from the passage.

TEACHING SOURCE TO COMPARE

Genesis Explained:

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLtS4zDr2sp3SH60RLpYwfnoKXXLk22UOu

Select a relevant lesson, record its title and timestamp, and compare one claim with your reading. Your own passage analysis is the learning evidence.

INTEGRITY PRACTICE

Acknowledge help from people or AI tools. Verify every cited source yourself. Your interpretation and oral explanation must be your own. Keep uncertainties visible and revise when the evidence changes.

SAVE IN YOUR PORTFOLIO

The source record, your analysis and the question for further study. Continue to SEM-000.4, Assemble your first portfolio.

FORMAT

Use a text document, an accessible PDF or an agreed alternative format. Keep a copy of your own work. When an instructor is assigned, use the designated private submission route for feedback. Keep private identifying information and personal pastoral disclosures out of public discussion threads.

PROPOSED COMPLETION CRITERIA

Aim for at least 8/10 on the study check after corrections. An instructor reviews the portfolio on five dimensions:

• Accurate program-status language.

• Accurate canon mapping.

• A traceable source record.

• Separation of observation and interpretation.

• A workable study plan.

Each dimension is scored 0–3: 0 missing; 1 developing; 2 competent; 3 strong. Proposed completion requires at least 12/15 overall with no dimension below 2. You have a revision opportunity after feedback. These criteria are prepared for academic review; a completion decision requires the instructor’s recorded review.

TORAH AND ISRAEL’S BEGINNINGS

SEM-101 pilot study guide | OT-01–OT-08 | 40 estimated study hours

Begin after SEM-000. This authored noncredit syllabus is prepared for academic review; reading-edition approval and instructor assignment are still to be finalized for an assessed cohort.

YOUR LEARNING GOALS

Explore creation, covenant, liberation, holiness, land, leadership and belonging through Genesis, Exodus, Leviticus, Numbers, Deuteronomy, Joshua, Judges and Ruth. Make a traceable record for every book, compare passages in context, explain an interpretive difficulty fairly, and design a short teaching session.

FOUR LEARNING BLOCKS

1. Creation, promise and liberation — Genesis and Exodus

Read Genesis 1–3; 12:1–9; 15; 22. Read Exodus 1–3; 12–14; 19–20.

Question: How do narrative and covenant shape identity and responsibility?

2. Holiness, community and the wilderness — Leviticus and Numbers

Read Leviticus 16; 19; 25. Read Numbers 11; 13–14; 27:1–11.

Question: How do these texts connect worship, communal life and leadership?

3. Memory, land and covenant choice — Deuteronomy and Joshua

Read Deuteronomy 6; 10:12–22; 15; 30. Read Joshua 1; 2; 24.

Question: How do remembering and retelling shape a community’s decisions?

4. Leadership, vulnerability and belonging — Judges and Ruth

Read Judges 2:6–23; 4–5; 6:1–24; 21:25. Read Ruth 1–4.

Question: How do narrative form and contrasting characters complicate a simple moral lesson?

Plan approximately 10 hours for each block: reading and annotation, selected teaching, discussion, preparing your work and revision. These are survey readings; full-book reading is an additional extension. Identify the legally accessible translation you use.

YOUR FIRST GUIDED ACTIVITY

Read Genesis 1:1–2:3 twice before consulting a commentary. Note the sequence, repeated language, transitions, portrayal of humanity and function of the ending. Write three observations with verse references. Develop one interpretation supported by at least two observations.

Compare Genesis 12:1–9 with Exodus 19:1–8. For each passage identify the speaker, addressee, promise or instruction, and response. Explain one connection and one difference. In 150–200 words respond to the claim: “The same covenant theme appears in both passages, so their settings do not matter.” Use one textual detail from each passage.

TEACHING SOURCE

Use Genesis Explained as a source to compare with your primary reading. Record the lesson title and relevant timestamps:

https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLtS4zDr2sp3SH60RLpYwfnoKXXLk22UOu

BUILD YOUR EIGHT-BOOK PORTFOLIO

For each book, record the assigned passages and edition; write a 250–350-word analysis; identify one unresolved question; and explain a 75–100-word application.

Also prepare a 700–900-word comparison using three passages from at least two books, and a 10-minute teaching lesson with a one-page facilitator guide. The full syllabus supplies block activities, the assessment plan and the reading-and-interpretation study check.

PROPOSED ASSESSMENT

Eight book worksheets: 40%. Comparative essay: 25%. Teaching demonstration and guide: 15%. Study check: 10%. Documented discussion and revision reflection: 10%. Proposed completion requires 80% overall, all eight worksheets, and competent textual accuracy and source traceability, with instructor feedback and revision.
