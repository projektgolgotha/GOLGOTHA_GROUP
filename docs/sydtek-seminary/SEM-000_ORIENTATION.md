# SEM-000 — Start Here: How to Study at SydTek Seminary

**Authored curriculum version:** 0.1, 30 October 2026. **Format:** noncredit orientation. **Planning estimate:** eight study hours. Part of the developing ECS-300 Ethiopian Canon Studies pathway.

## Welcome

SydTek Seminary is developing a structured study pathway across the Ethiopian biblical tradition. Your starting task is to learn how the curriculum is organized, how to read and evaluate sources, and how to demonstrate your own learning.

**Program status:** This curriculum is in development for academic review. It is presently noncredit, with no claim of accreditation, degree authority, transferable credit, or ordination. Assessed completion requires the published academic requirements and an instructor's documented review.

Optional donations support the educational work. Assessment is based on your learning.

## What you will be able to do

By the end of orientation, you should be able to:

1. Explain the curriculum's declared 46 Old Testament plus 35 New Testament numbering basis and identify two examples of grouped or divided texts.
2. Distinguish a primary text, a translation, scholarly interpretation, and a teaching video.
3. Prepare a source record that makes a passage and edition traceable.
4. Produce a realistic study plan and a small evidence portfolio while distinguishing study-hour estimates from academic credits.

## Lesson 1 — Your pathway and study plan

**Suggested time:** two hours, including reading, writing, and review.

Read the welcome above and the course list in the Seminary classroom. The planned sequence is orientation; Torah and Israel's beginnings; history and restoration; Enoch, Jubilees, and Meqabyan; wisdom and prayer; major prophets; the twelve prophets; Gospels and Acts; Pauline letters and Hebrews; general epistles and Revelation; Ethiopian church order; and a final capstone.

The whole-canon pathway is a survey. Each required registry unit needs a mapped reading, an assessed response, and an instructor-reviewed completion record. A survey uses carefully selected passages; it should not be described as reading every word of every text unless the individual learner actually completes that separate requirement.

**Activity:** Write your reason for studying, your preferred language, the days and hours you can realistically study, and one accessibility or support need. Choose an initial four-week schedule. Record an estimate now; revise it after your first two weeks of actual work.

**Discussion:** Which is more useful for evaluating your learning: how many videos you opened, or the quality of a passage analysis you can explain? Give one reason and one example. Respond respectfully to a peer if a moderated discussion is available.

## Lesson 2 — Reading the 81-entry map

**Suggested time:** two hours.

Open the [Ethiopian Orthodox Tewahedo Church's published canon list](https://ethiopianorthodox.org/english/canonical/books.html). Our working registry follows its 46 Old Testament and 35 New Testament entries. The [Mahibere Kidusan Holy Scriptures page](https://eotcmk.org/e/holy-scriptures/) provides a corresponding list.

The unit IDs are OT-01 through OT-46 and NT-01 through NT-35. A registry entry is a counting unit: OT-09 includes both 1 and 2 Samuel. It is not a claim that every entry has the same length or difficulty. The English list's Maccabees labels must be connected to Ethiopic Meqabyan texts, not substituted with the Greek books of Maccabees. The [United Bible Societies translation resource](https://tips.translation.bible/story/the-book-of-1-meqabyan/) explains that distinction.

Four Sinodos entries and two Covenant entries require attention to the individual part being studied. A general playlist about church order does not automatically document all those parts. The curriculum will also use supplemental historical or comparative materials; those materials need their own labels.

**Activity:** Find OT-09, OT-20, OT-21, and NT-28 through NT-35. Create a short table with the entry ID, the title used in the church list, and one question you would ask before choosing a translation. Keep the original source label alongside the classroom label.

**Reflection:** Explain why two lists can both use the number 81 yet need a careful crosswalk before we compare their coverage. You are not required to settle every historical counting convention in this orientation.

## Lesson 3 — Build a traceable reading record

**Suggested time:** two hours.

For a passage, distinguish four layers:

- **Text:** the work and passage you are studying.
- **Edition or translation:** the specific form of that work you can inspect.
- **Interpretation:** an explanation or argument about the passage.
- **Application:** how you propose to respond in life, teaching, or ministry.

A video is a teaching source. Check its claims against a primary passage and a suitable reference. If the video has no source for a historical claim, record the claim as needing verification. Where translations differ, quote only what is necessary, identify the edition, and explain the effect of the difference.

**Activity:** Use Genesis 1:1–2:3 in a legally accessible translation you already own or can access. Record its title, translator or publishing body, edition/year if supplied, passage range, URL or page number, and access date. Write 150–200 words separating three observations about the passage from one interpretation. Add one question that you cannot yet answer.

**Integrity practice:** Acknowledge help from people or AI tools. Verify every cited source yourself. Your submitted interpretation and oral explanation must be your own. Do not invent a quotation, source, reading log, or completion record.

## Lesson 4 — Assemble your first portfolio

**Suggested time:** two hours.

Submit an orientation portfolio containing:

1. Your four-week study plan, with estimated weekly hours and a revision date.
2. Your canon-mapping table from Lesson 2.
3. Your passage record and analysis from Lesson 3.
4. A 150–200-word reflection explaining how your reading evidence demonstrates learning and where your understanding still needs development.
5. The study check below, followed by a short correction note for any question you missed.

Use a text document, an accessible PDF, or an agreed alternative format. Where an instructor is assigned, request feedback through the specified private submission route. Do not post private identifying information or personal pastoral disclosures in public discussion threads.

## Study check — attempt before reading the answer key

1. Which basis does this registry follow? A: A 39-book OT and 27-book NT list. B: The identified church's 46 OT and 35 NT entries. C: A 54-book OT and 27-book NT list without a crosswalk. D: Alphabetical ordering of all available playlists.
2. OT-09 combines two Samuel books. What should your reading record preserve? A: Only the combined title. B: Only the book you studied first. C: The counting unit and evidence for both component texts. D: Two new canon numbers replacing the adopted entry.
3. Which source record lets a reviewer reproduce your reading? A: Work, topic, and your reaction. B: Author and a search-engine result. C: Video title and upload date alone. D: Work, edition, passage, publisher or URL/page, and access date.
4. Before assigning a Maccabees resource to a Meqabyan unit, what matters most? A: Matching the English numeral. B: Establishing the identity of the actual Ethiopic work. C: Matching the number of chapters. D: Choosing the most widely used Western translation.
5. What should you do after your first two weeks take longer than planned? A: Revise the workload estimate using your actual experience. B: Keep the original estimate as a record of actual time. C: Omit difficult readings from the record. D: Treat the difference as academic credit earned.
6. A lecture and a reference work give different historical dates. What is the best next step? A: Select the more confident speaker. B: Average the dates. C: Compare their sources and record the uncertainty. D: Use whichever date appears in more comments.
7. Which statement is an observation rather than an interpretation? A: The text teaches my preferred political system. B: This passage settles every question about creation. C: The author intended my modern application. D: The passage repeats a particular phrase at several points.
8. A commentary gives an interesting reading of Genesis. How should you use it? A: Identify its argument, examine the cited passage, and distinguish it from your conclusion. B: Treat the commentary as the primary passage. C: Cite only the commentator's credentials. D: Adopt the conclusion without recording its reasoning.
9. A required Ethiopic edition has not yet been identified. What should your record show? A: A substitute text with no explanation. B: The unresolved identity and the review needed. C: A completed reading based on its title. D: An omitted unit with no note.
10. What makes a teaching portfolio persuasive? A: The largest possible list of topics. B: A polished presentation without sources. C: Traceable analysis, reasoned choices, and revisions after feedback. D: A transcript of a lecture without your own analysis.

**Answer key:** 1 B; 2 C; 3 D; 4 B; 5 A; 6 C; 7 D; 8 A; 9 B; 10 C.

## Proposed completion criteria

Achieve at least 8/10 on the study check after corrections. The instructor reviews the portfolio on five dimensions: accurate status language; accurate canon mapping; traceable source record; separation of observation and interpretation; and a workable study plan. Each dimension is scored 0–3: 0 missing, 1 developing, 2 competent, 3 strong. Proposed completion requires at least 12/15 overall with no dimension below 2, with a revision opportunity after feedback.

## Instructor implementation note

Before enrolling an assessed cohort, name the instructor, confirm the private submission route, publish the response-time commitment and assessment appeal route, and verify accessibility of required readings. Record the learner ID, curriculum version, assessment date, reviewer, rubric scores, feedback, revision, and completion decision. A public Skool comment or automated lesson tick is insufficient as the entire academic record.
