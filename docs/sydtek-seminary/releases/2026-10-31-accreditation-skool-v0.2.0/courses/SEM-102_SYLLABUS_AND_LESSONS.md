# SEM-102 — Israel’s History and Restoration

**Version:** 0.1, 31 October 2026. **Pathway:** ECS-300 Ethiopian Canon Studies development track. **Format:** proposed noncredit survey. **Planning estimate:** 44 study hours across four learning blocks. **Prerequisite:** SEM-101 or equivalent reading and source-record skills. This syllabus is an authored draft awaiting academic adoption, faculty review, approved editions, accessibility review, and instructor assignment.

## Course description

Study monarchy, covenant leadership, exile, restoration, communal identity, and the transmission of historical traditions through the Seminary registry units OT-09–OT-12, OT-15–OT-19, and OT-46. The course treats grouped counting units honestly: 1–2 Samuel, 1–2 Kings, and Ezra–Nehemiah require evidence for every component, while Ezra II/Ezra Sutuel and Joseph ben Gorion/Josippon require faculty-approved identity and edition records before assessed teaching.

## Learning outcomes

After completing the course, learners should be able to:

1. Locate all ten registry units and every component text in the adopted 81-item manifest.
2. Explain how narrative selection, chronology, and theological interpretation shape accounts of monarchy, exile, and restoration.
3. Compare parallel or related passages without assuming that different works have identical purposes or historical settings.
4. Document edition and identity uncertainty for Ezra/Esdras traditions, Esther additions, and Josippon rather than silently resolving it.
5. Produce a source-based teaching or research artifact that distinguishes primary text, historical proposal, theological interpretation, and contemporary application.

## Required materials and source controls

Learners identify the edition or translation used for every reading. Before an assessed cohort begins, faculty must approve the reading list for common biblical books and separately approve the text identity for Ezra II, Ezra Sutuel, Esther’s textual extent, and Joseph ben Gorion/Josippon. A general Josephus source is not automatically evidence of Josippon coverage.

Each learner maintains a course source ledger with: registry ID, component, edition, translator/editor where available, access route, reading range, teaching source, timestamps, rights note, uncertainty note, and faculty approval state.

## Block 1 — Monarchy and covenant leadership

### Close-reading activity

Create a four-column record for each assigned episode: narrator’s description, character speech, action/consequence, and interpretive question. Compare 1 Samuel 8 with 2 Samuel 7, then compare 1 Kings 11–12 with 2 Kings 17. Identify where the text evaluates leadership explicitly and where the learner is inferring an evaluation.

### Assignment

Submit component worksheets for 1 Samuel, 2 Samuel, 1 Kings, and 2 Kings. Write a 700–900-word analysis explaining one continuity and one tension in the portrayal of kingship. Support the claim with at least four passage references and one appropriately identified secondary source.

## Block 2 — Rewriting history and communal memory

Compare one Chronicles passage with its Samuel/Kings parallel. Do not treat differences as mistakes or harmonize them automatically. Ask what the selection, omission, order, and emphasis may accomplish for the later work’s audience.

**Assignment:** Produce a parallel-passage matrix and a 500–700-word interpretation memo. Include the passages, observed differences, a cautious explanation, an alternative explanation, and the evidence still needed.

## Block 3 — Return, identity, and contested preservation

Read Ezra and Nehemiah as related but distinct works. Identify who speaks, who acts, and which groups are affected. For contested or ethically difficult material, represent the text accurately, avoid using ancient boundary language to target living communities, and identify the interpretive steps required before contemporary application.

Before using OT-16, the instructor publishes an edition crosswalk showing exactly what text the class is reading and why. Learners may not substitute a differently numbered Esdras work without recording the difference.

**Assignment:** Submit Ezra and Nehemiah worksheets, an Esther edition note, and an OT-16 identity memo. Then write a 900–1,100-word comparative essay on communal restoration and boundary-making. Include an ethical reflection and one plausible alternative interpretation.

## Block 4 — Deliverance traditions and historical writing

Read Tobit and Judith with attention to literary form, prayer, providence, agency, and communal memory. For OT-46, distinguish Josippon/Zena Ayhud from Flavius Josephus and record the approved edition.

**Teaching/research task:** Create either a 12-minute teaching session with a facilitator guide or a 1,200–1,500-word research brief. The artifact must compare two course works, identify genre and source limits, and include a source ledger. Submit a 200-word revision reflection after feedback.

## Study check

1. A source registry groups 1 and 2 Samuel as one numbered unit. What must a learner’s portfolio show? A: Evidence from both component books. B: Evidence from either one. C: Only a video title. D: No source record.
2. A Chronicles passage differs from a Samuel/Kings parallel. The best first step is to: A: erase the difference; B: observe and document the difference in context; C: assume bad faith; D: treat both as identical summaries.
3. Which claim requires an edition record? A: Ezra/Esdras numbering and identity. B: A page number in the learner’s notebook only. C: The course title. D: The learner’s preferred font.
4. Why is a general Flavius Josephus playlist insufficient for OT-46? A: Josippon/Zena Ayhud is a distinct source identity that must be verified. B: Historical sources are prohibited. C: Videos can never teach. D: OT-46 has no text.
5. A modern application of an ancient boundary-making narrative should: A: target a living group directly; B: skip historical context; C: explain the interpretive and ethical reasoning between text and application; D: rely on confidence alone.
6. A source ledger should distinguish: A: primary text, secondary source, teaching source, and learner interpretation. B: only public views. C: only the instructor’s name. D: only the final grade.
7. The strongest parallel-passage memo includes: A: observed differences, a cautious explanation, an alternative, and needed evidence. B: one quotation without context. C: a conclusion before reading. D: no references.
8. Which statement is responsible? A: “The current evidence does not resolve this edition identity.” B: “All numbered Ezra works are the same.” C: “My preferred title proves the source.” D: “Uncertainty should be hidden.”
9. What distinguishes narrative description from command? A: A reader must argue from genre, context, and broader reasoning before treating an action as normative. B: Every action is automatically commanded. C: Narratives contain no theology. D: Commands never occur in narratives.
10. A revision reflection demonstrates learning when it: A: identifies how evidence changed the claim or scope; B: says only “done”; C: removes all criticism; D: reports only word count.

**Answer key:** 1 A; 2 B; 3 A; 4 A; 5 C; 6 A; 7 A; 8 A; 9 A; 10 A. For assessed use, separate the key and require explanations for selected answers.
