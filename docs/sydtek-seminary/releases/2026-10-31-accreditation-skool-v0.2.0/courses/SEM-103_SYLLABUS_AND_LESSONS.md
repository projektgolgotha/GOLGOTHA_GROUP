# SEM-103 — Enoch, Jubilees, and Meqabyan

**Version:** 0.1, 31 October 2026. **Pathway:** ECS-300 Ethiopian Canon Studies development track. **Format:** proposed noncredit survey. **Planning estimate:** 32 study hours across four learning blocks. **Prerequisite:** SEM-101 and source-ledger orientation. This authored draft awaits academic adoption, approved translations, faculty review, accessibility review, and instructor assignment.

## Course description

Study Jubilees, Ethiopic 1 Enoch, and the Ethiopic Meqabyan traditions through selected passages, source-history records, literary analysis, and comparison. The course preserves the Seminary registry’s counting convention: OT-13 Jubilees, OT-14 1 Enoch, OT-20 1 Meqabyan, and OT-21 2 and 3 Meqabyan. It explicitly prohibits substituting 2 Enoch, 3 Enoch, the Book of Giants, or Greek 1–3 Maccabees for the assigned Ethiopic works.

## Learning outcomes

Learners should be able to:

1. Identify each assigned work and distinguish it from similarly named but different texts.
2. Describe selected themes—covenant, calendar, judgment, heavenly revelation, faithful resistance, and moral accountability—using traceable passage evidence.
3. Compare a selected passage with a related biblical text without claiming that similarity establishes identical authorship, date, or authority.
4. Explain source, translation, edition, and access limitations honestly.
5. Produce an original comparative teaching or research artifact that represents each tradition fairly and documents uncertainty.

## Source and rights gate

Before assessed delivery, faculty approves legally usable editions or excerpts for all four registry units. Every learner records edition, translator/editor, publication data, access route, passage/chapter range, and any limitations. English availability varies, especially for Meqabyan; absence of an approved accessible edition means the related assessed activity remains paused rather than replaced with a different work.

## Block 1 — Jubilees

Read faculty-approved selections that cover creation/ordering, covenant, and calendar or festival interpretation. Build a three-column comparison: source tradition recalled, Jubilees’ retelling or expansion, and interpretive effect. Do not assume a direct one-to-one relationship without documenting the passages.

**Assignment:** A 600–800-word rewritten-tradition analysis plus a source ledger. Include one alternative explanation and a short note on how the selected edition affects the analysis.

## Block 2 — Ethiopic 1 Enoch

The instructor identifies the sections and chapter numbering in the approved edition. Learners distinguish Ethiopic 1 Enoch from 2 Enoch, 3 Enoch, and the Book of Giants. Use genre labels cautiously and note that the work contains multiple major sections.

**Assignment:** Create a section map and write an 800–1,000-word interpretation of one theme across at least two approved selections. Compare one related biblical passage, explaining both the connection and the limits of the comparison.

**Discussion:** What can a literary or thematic connection establish, and what can it not establish about date, dependence, canon, or doctrine?

## Block 3 — 1 Meqabyan

Begin with an identity note: Ethiopic 1 Meqabyan is not Greek 1 Maccabees. The instructor records the approved translation and chapter scheme. Analyze how the text portrays idolatry, resistance, leadership, and divine judgment.

**Assignment:** A 500–700-word character/argument study supported by passage references. Add a 150-word distinction memo explaining why Greek Maccabees cannot substitute for the assigned work.

## Block 4 — 2 and 3 Meqabyan

Treat 2 and 3 Meqabyan as separate component works within one registry counting unit. The learner portfolio must contain evidence for both. Compare their treatment of mortality, accountability, resurrection, faithful memory, or ethical teaching using approved selections.

**Final artifact:** Choose one:

- a 12-minute teaching session with facilitator guide;
- a 1,200–1,500-word comparative research brief; or
- a curated digital exhibit with 1,000–1,200 words of original interpretation.

The artifact must distinguish all assigned works, include a rights/source log, represent one plausible alternative interpretation, and state at least two limitations.

## Study check

1. Which work satisfies OT-14? A: Ethiopic 1 Enoch. B: 2 Enoch. C: 3 Enoch. D: Book of Giants.
2. Why must the edition of 1 Enoch be recorded? A: Section and chapter numbering can vary, and the source must be traceable. B: Editions are irrelevant. C: A video title replaces the text. D: Only page color matters.
3. A comparison between Jubilees and Genesis can establish: A: observed similarities and differences in selected passages. B: identical authorship automatically. C: identical canonical status in every tradition. D: an exact date without other evidence.
4. Ethiopic 1 Meqabyan is: A: distinct from Greek 1 Maccabees. B: another title for Greek 1 Maccabees. C: a chapter of 1 Enoch. D: the same as Jubilees.
5. OT-21 requires evidence from: A: both 2 Meqabyan and 3 Meqabyan. B: either one. C: Greek 2 Maccabees only. D: no primary text.
6. The responsible response to a missing approved translation is to: A: pause or redesign the assessed activity and document the gap. B: silently substitute another work. C: invent quotations. D: claim full coverage from a playlist title.
7. A source distinction memo should identify: A: title, tradition, edition, and why a similarly named text is different. B: only the learner’s preference. C: only view counts. D: only a grade.
8. Which claim needs further evidence? A: “These passages share a judgment theme.” B: “The similarity proves identical date and authorship.” C: “The selected edition labels this section.” D: “The texts use different character names.”
9. The final artifact must include limitations because: A: responsible interpretation states what the evidence does not establish. B: limitations weaken all research. C: uncertainty should be hidden. D: a confident tone removes source gaps.
10. An oral/source verification primarily helps confirm: A: the learner can explain the work, sources, reasoning, and AI use. B: the learner memorized a marketing slogan. C: the public view count. D: the course-card color.

**Answer key:** 1 A; 2 A; 3 A; 4 A; 5 A; 6 A; 7 A; 8 B; 9 A; 10 A. Separate the key for assessed use.
