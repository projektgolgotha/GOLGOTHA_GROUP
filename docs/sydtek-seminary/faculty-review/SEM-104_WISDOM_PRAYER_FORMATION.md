# SEM-104 — Wisdom, Prayer, and Formation

**Version:** 0.1 faculty-review draft, 31 October 2026  
**Pathway:** ECS-300 Ethiopian Canon Studies, proposed 81-book delivery sequence  
**Format:** Proposed noncredit genre and formation survey  
**Planning estimate:** 36 study hours across four learning blocks  
**Prerequisite:** SEM-000 and SEM-101  
**Status:** Not faculty approved; exact editions, Reproof/Tegsats crosswalk, Wisdom text identity, readings, and assessment rules remain under review

## Course description

Examine suffering, praise, lament, instruction, love, mortality, justice, and spiritual formation through Job, Psalms, Proverbs/Messale, Reproof/Tegsats, Wisdom/Metsihafe Tibeb, Ecclesiastes, Song of Songs, and Sirach. The course trains learners to interpret different genres on their own terms rather than flattening poetry, wisdom instruction, dialogue, lament, and love song into one form of teaching.

The course maps to **OT-22–OT-28 and OT-45**. The registry currently treats **Proverbs/Messale** and **Reproof/Tegsats** as separate counted entries. A proposed Proverbs 1–24 / Proverbs 25–31 crosswalk is a candidate only and must be verified against the adopted edition. **Wisdom/Metsihafe Tibeb** also requires exact text and edition confirmation before assessed delivery.

## Proposed alignment

Primary program outcomes: **PLO-1 Canon literacy, PLO-2 Interpretation, PLO-3 Ethiopian context, PLO-4 Research integrity, PLO-5 Teaching, and PLO-6 Reflective practice**.

## Proposed course learning outcomes

After completing the adopted course, a learner should be able to:

1. Account for all eight registry units and explain the approved relationship between Proverbs/Messale and Reproof/Tegsats.
2. Interpret a wisdom, lament, praise, dialogue, philosophical reflection, or love-poetry passage with attention to genre and literary context.
3. Compare at least two approaches to suffering, prayer, moral formation, or human limitation without forcing them into identical conclusions.
4. Distinguish textual observation from theological synthesis, pastoral counsel, and contemporary application.
5. Produce a source-aware formation activity that includes a measurable learner outcome and safeguards against harmful simplification.
6. Revise an interpretation after feedback and explain why the revision better fits the text and the learner context.

## Proposed block activities

### Block 1 — Resist the easy answer

Map the voices in Job 1–3 and 38–42. Distinguish narrator statements, character claims, divine speech, and later interpretation. Compare one Job passage with a lament psalm, identifying what each text permits the reader to say and what it does not resolve.

**Submission:** Job and Psalms worksheets plus a 600–800-word comparison entitled “What the text does not let me simplify.”

### Block 2 — Formation through instruction

Compare a sustained instruction in Proverbs or Sirach with several short sayings. Identify audience, method, repeated vocabulary, social assumptions, and the kind of formation being pursued.

For Reproof/Tegsats, submit an identity and chapter-crosswalk record before interpreting it. If the crosswalk is not approved, the unit remains a research problem rather than claimed coverage.

**Submission:** Proverbs/Messale, Reproof/Tegsats, and Sirach unit records; a one-page formation map; and a 500–700-word analysis of how instruction becomes practice.

### Block 3 — Wisdom and human limits

Interpret one approved passage from Wisdom/Metsihafe Tibeb and one passage from Ecclesiastes. Explain the argument, imagery, and view of human limitation. Do not assume that the two books use the same voice or resolve mortality in the same way.

**Submission:** two unit worksheets and an 800–1,000-word comparative essay that presents a plausible alternative reading fairly.

### Block 4 — Poetry, love, and teaching responsibility

Read Song of Songs according to the faculty-approved scope. Compare literal, literary, theological, and reception-oriented questions without using an unstated interpretation as the only acceptable academic answer.

**Teaching task:** design a 12-minute adult-learning activity using one poetic text and one wisdom or prayer text. State a measurable outcome, include a learner choice or accessible alternative, avoid pressuring personal disclosure, and explain the limits of the application.

**Submission:** Song of Songs worksheet, facilitator guide, teaching demonstration, feedback, and a 200–300-word revision reflection.

## Proposed study-check domains

The study check should evaluate whether a learner can:

- identify genre and speaker before applying a passage;
- distinguish lament from unbelief or failure without erasing the text’s tension;
- explain why Job’s characters cannot all be quoted as the narrator’s final position;
- document psalm numbering and translation;
- explain the provisional status of Reproof/Tegsats and Wisdom/Metsihafe Tibeb;
- distinguish Sirach from Ecclesiastes;
- compare a proverb with sustained instruction;
- identify when a pastoral application exceeds the explicit textual claim.

A final question bank should be created only after readings and editions are approved.

## Instruction and learner support

Texts on suffering, death, sexuality, embodiment, abuse of authority, and unanswered prayer can affect learners personally. The course should not require disclosure of trauma or intimate experience. The instructor should provide content notices where appropriate, an alternate way to demonstrate the learning outcome, and a referral route for pastoral or professional support outside grading.

The instructor must distinguish academic evaluation from spiritual surveillance. Learners are assessed on textual reasoning, evidence, fair representation, and the published learning outcomes—not on disclosing private experiences or agreeing with an unstated devotional conclusion.
