# SEM-103 — Enoch, Jubilees, and Meqabyan

**Version:** 0.1 faculty-review draft, 31 October 2026  
**Pathway:** ECS-300 Ethiopian Canon Studies, proposed 81-book delivery sequence  
**Format:** Proposed noncredit text-identity and comparative-reading course  
**Planning estimate:** 32 study hours across four learning blocks  
**Prerequisite:** SEM-000 and SEM-101; SEM-102 recommended  
**Status:** Not faculty approved; exact Ethiopic works, editions, passage crosswalks, teaching sources, and assessment rules remain under review

## Course description

Study **Jubilees, Ethiopic 1 Enoch, 1 Meqabyan, and the grouped 2–3 Meqabyan entry** with unusual attention to textual identity. Learners will build bibliographic records, read approved primary passages, compare themes across distinct works, and demonstrate why a familiar English title cannot be treated as proof that the correct Ethiopic work has been assigned.

The course maps to **OT-13, OT-14, OT-20, and OT-21**. OT-21 contains two required components, so the course accounts for five textual components across four numbered canon entries.

This course must explicitly distinguish:

- **1 Enoch** from 2 Enoch, 3 Enoch, the Book of Giants, and generic “Enoch” material;
- **1–3 Meqabyan** from Greek 1–4 Maccabees;
- a teaching playlist about a topic from an academically verified primary-text assignment.

## Proposed alignment

Primary program outcomes: **PLO-1 Canon literacy, PLO-2 Interpretation, PLO-3 Ethiopian context, PLO-4 Research integrity, PLO-5 Teaching, and PLO-6 Reflective practice**.

## Proposed course learning outcomes

After completing the adopted course, a learner should be able to:

1. Identify each required work and explain the title or numbering confusion that can lead to a false source match.
2. Produce a reproducible edition record for Jubilees, 1 Enoch, and each Meqabyan component used in the course.
3. Interpret selected passages in literary context without collapsing distinct texts into one theological summary.
4. Compare a theme—such as covenant, judgment, rebellion, wisdom, suffering, faithfulness, or resurrection—across two approved works using traceable evidence.
5. Evaluate a teaching source by checking its claims against the assigned primary text and identifying corrections or limits.
6. Present the reception or use of one work without implying that every course interpretation is an official Ethiopian Orthodox position.

## Proposed block activities

### Block 1 — Build the identity dossier

For each work, create a one-page identity record containing:

- registry ID and display title;
- title on the assigned edition;
- language and translation information;
- editor, translator, publisher, date, and stable access route;
- table of contents or chapter structure;
- alternate titles and works that must not be confused with it;
- known limits of the edition or translation;
- status of the candidate teaching source.

**Stop rule:** if the reviewer cannot establish the actual work and edition, the course may discuss the identification problem but may not claim assessed coverage of that component.

### Block 2 — Jubilees as rewritten tradition

Compare one approved Jubilees passage with the corresponding passage or theme in Genesis or Exodus. Identify what is retained, expanded, reordered, or interpreted. Explain the comparison without treating either text as a simple paraphrase of the other.

**Submission:** Jubilees unit worksheet, edition record, and a 600–800-word comparative analysis.

### Block 3 — Literary sections of 1 Enoch

Map the assigned readings to their literary section. The learner should not cite “the Book of Enoch” as though every statement comes from one undifferentiated composition. Record the section, passage, speaker, imagery, and interpretive question.

**Submission:** 1 Enoch unit worksheet; a section map; and a 700–900-word interpretation of one assigned passage that explains the limits of its claim.

### Block 4 — Meqabyan without substitution

Complete separate evidence records for 1, 2, and 3 Meqabyan. Compare one theme across at least two works only after documenting that the passages come from the approved Ethiopic texts. A Greek Maccabees passage may be used for an explicitly labeled comparative exercise, but it cannot satisfy the Meqabyan reading requirement.

**Submission:** three component records; a 900–1,200-word comparative essay; and a five-minute oral source check in which the learner explains the edition and text identity of the cited passages.

Proposed completion threshold: 80% overall, all five component records, and at least competent performance in textual accuracy and evidence/traceability. A learner who uses the wrong text family for a required component must correct the record before completion, regardless of the numeric total.

## Teaching-source audit

Each learner or faculty reviewer should select one candidate video and document:

1. exact title, URL, date accessed, and timestamps used;
2. the primary passage or work it claims to explain;
3. three claims checked against the approved text;
4. any conflation of 1 Enoch with another Enoch work or Meqabyan with Greek Maccabees;
5. interpretations that should be labeled as the instructor’s position;
6. accessibility, citation, and copyright concerns;
7. final disposition: approved segment, approved with correction, contextual only, or excluded.

## Proposed study-check domains

The course study check should evaluate whether a learner can:

- identify 1 Enoch as the intended Enoch work in the registry;
- explain why numbering alone does not make Meqabyan and Greek Maccabees interchangeable;
- distinguish a text’s identity from a claim about its date, authorship, or reception;
- identify the literary section of an assigned 1 Enoch passage;
- document a translation or edition;
- compare a retelling with its source tradition without erasing differences;
- state uncertainty when evidence is incomplete;
- explain why a video title is not proof of coverage.

The question bank should be written only after faculty approves the editions and readings.

## Instruction and learner support

Because the course includes apocalyptic imagery, violence, persecution, judgment, and contested religious identity, the instructor should establish discussion norms and distinguish academic analysis from sensational prediction. Learners should be given an accessible route for questions about unfamiliar textual traditions and should receive feedback on the text-identity dossier before submitting the comparative essay.

The instructor should have demonstrated competence in biblical/apocryphal literature and the ability to verify the Ethiopic text tradition or should teach with an appropriately qualified reviewer. The course should not imply expertise in Geʿez when the instructor is working only through translations.
